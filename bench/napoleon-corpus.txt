Napoleon Bonaparte was born on 15 August 1769 in Ajaccio, on the island of Corsica.

Corsica had been ceded to France by the Republic of Genoa in 1768, the year before Napoleon was born, which is why he was born a French subject.

Napoleon's parents were Carlo Buonaparte, a lawyer, and Letizia Ramolino. He was their fourth child and second surviving son.

Napoleon was educated at the military academy of Brienne-le-Chateau and then at the Ecole Militaire in Paris. He was commissioned as a second lieutenant of artillery in 1785.

Napoleon first made his name at the Siege of Toulon in 1793, where his handling of the artillery forced a British withdrawal. He was promoted to brigadier general at the age of 24.

On 13 Vendemiaire, in October 1795, Napoleon dispersed a royalist insurrection in Paris with artillery fire, an episode later called the whiff of grapeshot.

Napoleon married Josephine de Beauharnais in March 1796. She was a widow of the Terror and six years his senior.

Napoleon commanded the Army of Italy from 1796, winning at Lodi, Arcole and Rivoli, and forcing Austria to the Treaty of Campo Formio in October 1797.

Napoleon invaded Egypt in 1798 and won the Battle of the Pyramids, but Admiral Horatio Nelson destroyed the French fleet at Aboukir Bay in August 1798, stranding the army.

The Rosetta Stone was discovered in 1799 by French soldiers during Napoleon's Egyptian campaign. It later provided the key to deciphering Egyptian hieroglyphs.

Napoleon seized power in the coup of 18 Brumaire in November 1799 and became First Consul of France.

The Napoleonic Code, a civil law code that abolished feudal privilege and guaranteed equality before the law, was promulgated in 1804. It remains the basis of civil law in many countries.

Napoleon crowned himself Emperor of the French on 2 December 1804 at Notre-Dame in Paris. Pope Pius VII was present but did not place the crown.

At the Battle of Trafalgar on 21 October 1805, Admiral Horatio Nelson destroyed the combined Franco-Spanish fleet, ending any hope of invading Britain. Nelson was killed in the battle.

The Battle of Austerlitz, fought on 2 December 1805, is considered Napoleon's greatest victory. It is also called the Battle of the Three Emperors.

Napoleon imposed the Continental System in 1806, an economic blockade intended to ruin Britain by closing European ports to British trade.

The Peninsular War began in 1808 when Napoleon installed his brother Joseph on the Spanish throne. The long guerrilla conflict that followed drained French strength and Napoleon called it the Spanish ulcer.

Napoleon divorced Josephine in 1810 because she had given him no heir, and married Marie Louise of Austria, a daughter of the Habsburg emperor.

Marie Louise bore Napoleon a son in 1811. The boy was styled King of Rome and is known to history as Napoleon II.

Napoleon invaded Russia in June 1812 with a Grande Armee of roughly 600,000 men, the largest army Europe had yet seen.

The Battle of Borodino was fought in September 1812. It was the bloodiest single day of the Napoleonic Wars and opened the road to Moscow.

Moscow burned after the French occupied it, and with no peace offered Napoleon began the retreat in October 1812. Cold, hunger and Cossack raids destroyed the army; fewer than one in ten men returned.

At the Battle of Leipzig in October 1813, also called the Battle of the Nations, a coalition of Russia, Prussia, Austria and Sweden defeated Napoleon and drove the French out of Germany.

Napoleon abdicated in April 1814 and was exiled to the island of Elba, off the coast of Italy, where he was allowed to keep the title of Emperor.

Napoleon escaped from Elba in February 1815 and returned to France, beginning the period known as the Hundred Days.

At the Battle of Waterloo on 18 June 1815, Napoleon was defeated by the Duke of Wellington and the Prussian army under Marshal Blucher.

After Waterloo, Napoleon was exiled to Saint Helena, a remote British island in the South Atlantic. He died there on 5 May 1821.

Napoleon's remains were returned to France in 1840 and entombed at Les Invalides in Paris.
